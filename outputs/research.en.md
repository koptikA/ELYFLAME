## Competitors

1. **Rhythmix Inc.** — a rhythmic gymnastics school in Buffalo Grove itself, with Recreational from age 3, Competitive levels 3–9, and a free first class — https://www.rhythmixinc.com/
2. **AP Rhythmic Academy & Stretching Studio** — the closest competitor by positioning, in neighboring Wheeling: kids from age 3, levels up to 6+, stretching and ballet, a free trial — https://aprhythmicacademy.com/
3. **North Shore Rhythmic Gymnastics Center** — a club in Glenview and Deerfield with Olympians and an elite competitive track, recreational from age 4 — https://www.northshorerhythmics.com/
4. **Vitrychenko Academy** — a club in Morton Grove and Arlington Heights under the brand of Olympic medalist Olena Vitrychenko, levels from age 2.5 — http://www.vitrychenkoacademy.com/
5. **Chicago Rhythmics** — a school across six partner gyms in Chicago, with published prices and a paid $15 trial — https://www.chicagorhythmics.com/
6. **Queen RG Club** — a club in Lake Barrington with published prices and online trial booking — https://www.queenrgclub.com/

## What Competitors Do Well

**Trust through named coaches and reviews (Rhythmix).** Rhythmix has 4.9★ from 30 Google reviews, 29 of them five stars. Parents praise coaches by name and describe their child's growing confidence, and the coaching family's 30-year history makes the school feel like "ours." It works because a parent's core question is who they're trusting their child with, and other parents' words answer it better than any claim from the school (https://www.trustindex.io/reviews/rhythmixinc.com).

**One action above the fold and a structure by level (AP Rhythmic Academy).** AP's site is the most modern of the group: a large "Book a free trial class now" CTA above the fold, the schedule in tabs by level, a competition calendar, its own Diamond Cup tournament, and a uniform shop. It works because a parent immediately sees what to do next and finds the right schedule without writing in.

**Credentials as the main argument for competitive families (North Shore Rhythmics, Vitrychenko).** NSR shows its Tokyo 2020 Olympians and the "2024 USA Gymnastics Rhythmic Coaches of the Year" title; Vitrychenko shows a 1996 Olympic bronze and nine world championship titles. For parents of girls heading into the sport, this is direct proof that the club delivers results.

**Transparent pricing, trial, and policies (Chicago Rhythmics).** Chicago Rhythmics publishes per-session prices ($208–348 for 8 weeks), explains the paid $15 trial in three steps, states "USAG Safety Certified and CPR Certified," and says plainly whether parents may enter the gym. It works because it removes the fear of hidden costs and an unsafe environment before the first call (https://www.chicagorhythmics.com/registration.html).

**Online trial booking (Queen RG Club).** Queen RG lets parents book a free try-out through a calendar and publishes monthly prices ($150–450). Among the clubs we found, it's the only one where the trial is booked online right on the site, without back-and-forth (https://www.queenrgclub.com/plans-pricing).

## What They're Missing

**No age-based class finder and no path for beginners.** No local competitor lets a parent enter a child's age and see the right class. NSR is built for elite athletes and barely explains where a 3-year-old should start, and Rhythmix buries its trial in the program text. Parents have to decode the levels themselves or call.

**Manual, fragmented sign-up.** AP switches to manual back-and-forth after the form; Chicago Rhythmics asks for an email, payment by Zelle or PayPal, and PDF forms; NSR sends sign-ups to park district sites; Vitrychenko has waivers signed on separate forms. No site takes a parent from choosing a class to a confirmed booking in one flow.

**The nearest competitors hide prices.** Rhythmix, AP, NSR, and Vitrychenko don't publish prices. Negative reviews of Vitrychenko mention an "abnormally strong obsession with money" and "they don't know how to teach" (https://local.yahoo.com/info-209869124-vitrychenko-academy-niles/): when prices aren't visible, the fear of overpaying becomes part of the reputation. At launch ElyFlame publishes only the prices in the SOW — the trial and Stretching — so we close this gap only partly.

**No one develops Stretching for other sports and adults.** AP has stretching only in its name; no one has a dedicated funnel for dancers, figure skaters, martial artists, and adults. It's an open audience with its own searches, like "flexibility class for dancers" and "adult stretching class near me."

**Dated template sites.** Competitor sites are built on Weebly, Wix, Weblium, and old WordPress. Vitrychenko's HTTPS certificate had expired when checked, and its footer reads "© 2015-2016." None has a Russian version, even though every club's coaches come from Ukraine, Belarus, Bulgaria, or Russia. A fast site with a premium look stands out on its own.

## Patterns We Borrow

**Age-based class finder — from Goldfish Swim School and North London RGA.** Goldfish offers "Take Our Swim Assessment" to parents who are "not sure what class is right for your child," and North London RGA groups children by birth year so parents don't have to decode levels. They aren't direct competitors, but their approach underpins the Champion's Path: age and experience → level 1–6 → Book a Trial with the program preselected.

**One primary button above the fold and on mobile — from AP Rhythmic Academy.** As at AP, Book a Trial is the only primary action above the fold. On top of that, the button stays on screen on mobile, next to tap-to-call. It fits because parents arrive on a phone from Instagram and Google Maps and decide within 1–3 visits.

**A step-by-step trial and a first-visit guide — from Chicago Rhythmics.** We borrow their clear description of the process and parent policies, minus the manual steps: a success screen with a summary and a "what to bring" list, and a first-class page covering the entrance, parking, and whether parents can watch. This eases the anxiety parents of little ones feel before the first visit.

**Credentials and safety next to the booking button — from NSR, Vitrychenko, and Chicago Rhythmics.** NSR's and Vitrychenko's credentials and Chicago Rhythmics' SafeSport and CPR become our "Coaches and safety" block. ElyFlame's strongest argument is that the founder is on the USA Gymnastics national judges' list: a judge knows exactly what earns points.

**Reviews naming coaches and collecting Google reviews — from Rhythmix.** We show 3–6 reviews with the parent's name and the child's program, and after the trial we send an email with a link to leave a Google review. Rhythmix's 30 reviews are a reachable bar, and reviews help local ranking (https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091).
