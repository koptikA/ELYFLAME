# Brief: ElyFlame Academy of Rhythmic Gymnastics Website

Written September 24, 2026, with the `/brief` command. Markers: *[hypothesis]* is a conclusion from the market research that the client hasn't confirmed yet; `[confirm: …]` means data we don't have yet. Sources: `docs/sow.md`, `docs/research/2026-09-24-market-research.md`, `docs/ideas.en.md`, `docs/decisions.en.md`.

## 1. Users

The core audience is parents of children under 8 in Buffalo Grove and the neighboring northwest Chicago suburbs who are looking for a first sport or a stronger program. They almost always open the site on a phone, arriving from Instagram or a Google Maps search. The second group is parents of girls 6+ who want a competitive path, including families ready to move from another club. The third, separate group is teens and adults from other sports (figure skaters, dancers, martial artists, runners) who need the Stretching & Flexibility class. The client still has to confirm which group matters most for filling classes `[confirm: question 1 for Kiryl]`. Until then, the focus follows the SOW: trial lessons for children.

*[hypothesis]* Finding a gym isn't the hard part: schools are visible on Google Maps and Instagram, and families will drive 18 miles for a good coach. The real pain is trusting a school when there's little information to go on. A parent of a 3–5-year-old worries the child will feel out of place, the coach will be too strict, or the first visit will end in tears. A parent of a competitive girl is choosing between clubs and fears a toxic culture and hidden costs; negative reviews of competitors literally mention an "obsession with money." The US context amplifies both fears. After the Larry Nassar scandal, child safety in gymnastics is a sensitive topic, and parents hold two stereotypes about Eastern European coaching at once: "it gets results" and "it's too harsh." ElyFlame should keep the first and dispel the second: judge-level technique with warmth toward the child.

*[hypothesis]* Trust builds differently for the two groups. Parents of little ones are most convinced by seeing how the coach works with children (class videos), then by other parents' words and clear safety measures such as SafeSport, CPR, and being allowed to watch class. Credentials are background for them. For parents of competitive girls, credentials come first: the founder is on the USA Gymnastics national judges' list. Next come a clear path to competition, athletes' results, and reviews that show a healthy atmosphere.

Parents use the site in short bursts. While a family is deciding, that's usually 1–3 visits over one or two weeks, followed by occasional visits to check the schedule, contacts, or policies. Today parents solve this by searching "rhythmic gymnastics near me," browsing gyms on Instagram, and asking friends and Russian-speaking parent chats (13.4% of Buffalo Grove residents and 17.7% in Wheeling speak a Slavic language at home). Competitors handle sign-ups with "leave a request and we'll call you" or a phone call.

## 2. Functionality

The main scenario: a parent finds the right class by the child's age and experience and books a trial lesson. The class finder is built into the Champion's Path. Levels 1–6 appear as a path from first steps at age 3 to serious competition; entering an age moves a spark to the matching level, and a Book a Trial button appears with that program preselected. Book a Trial is the site's only primary button. It appears on every page and stays on screen on mobile.

The project lead chooses how deep booking goes in v1 `[confirm: D1]`. Option A is a request with a callback, B is instant booking with a time slot and a $10 payment, and C is a hybrid: the parent picks a preferred day, and after the academy confirms, they get a payment link. Design recommends and is building for option C, because its screens are nearly identical to B's, so moving to B later won't require rework.

Supporting scenarios help the family decide and prepare. The parent explores the school: coaches, safety, the path to competition, and achievements. They check the schedule and the prices the SOW includes: the trial fee and the price on the Stretching page. They prepare for the first class: the building entrance, parking, what to bring, and whether parents can watch. They contact the school through the contact details and form, and they register after the trial. Adults and athletes book Stretching on a single page with a section per audience.

There is no Register button in the header, even though the SOW includes one. Registration only happens after a trial, so it's reachable from the post-trial email, an "Already had your trial? Register" block on Parents' Info, and the footer. Registration itself is part of the site (`/register`): parent and child details, class, waiver, and the first payment. Whether it's our own form or a link to an external platform is the client's call `[confirm: question 7]`. The parent portal login is a quiet link. Out of scope for v1: the parent portal (a separate 2027 project), a live Instagram feed (phase 2), a Russian version of the site (phase 2), and separate Stretching landing pages (phase 2). Per the SOW, the admin panel is part of project 1 (item 11); the project lead decides whether it's needed at launch `[confirm: D5]`.

## 3. Technical Constraints

The website is designed mobile-first and built with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript without frameworks, as the SOW requires. Forms must submit without JS; JS adds validation and the class finder. Wherever possible we use browser features instead of libraries: `<dialog>` for the booking modal, `<details>` for the FAQ, `scroll-snap` for testimonials, built-in form validation, and CSS scroll-driven animations for the Ribbon. GSAP comes in only if the native tools aren't enough. Type and spacing scales are calculated in Utopia and handed to CSS as tokens. SOW acceptance requirements: WCAG 2.1 AA and a mobile Lighthouse score of 90+ for Performance, Accessibility, and SEO.

Required integrations: form submission with emails to the academy and the parent, an embedded Google Map with Get Directions, a Google review link in post-trial emails, and a Stripe Payment Link for the trial fee (if D1 is B or C). A live Instagram feed in phase 2 will need a server-side function: the Basic Display API has been shut down since December 2024, and the new API's token can't be stored in the browser. Programs, ages, and the schedule live in a single config file that feeds the class finder and the forms.

The site stores trial requests and contact messages, including a child's name and age, and, if registration is our own form, registration details and the signed waiver. The project lead chooses where they're stored and who can access them, in line with the SOW's security requirements `[confirm: D3]`. The SOW doesn't include analytics with events on buttons and forms, and without it the metrics can't be measured `[confirm: D2]`. There are no user accounts at launch. Content is copy plus photos and videos of the athletes. For now, photos and videos exist only on Instagram; we've asked the client for originals and for parental consent to publish them.

## 4. UI/UX

The style is a restrained light base with one expressive signature motif. A thin Ribbon in the brand gradient (magenta #E9008D → orange #FDA63D) runs through the whole site and draws itself as the visitor scrolls. It echoes the curl of the E in the ElyFlame mark and one of the apparatus of rhythmic gymnastics. The Stage sets the rhythm of each page: selected sections (achievements, the "Now Enrolling" banner, performance videos) are dark, like a stage under spotlights, with a soft gradient glow. Motion explains what's happening rather than decorating. With the system "reduce motion" setting on, the Ribbon is a static line.

The tone is graceful, energetic, premium, and warm toward parents, never generic or corporate. Headings are set in Cinzel, which echoes the logo wordmark, so headings stay short. Body text is Manrope at 16 px and up. Buttons and fields are 44–48 px for touch. Contrast is checked separately: magenta on white is about 4.3:1, which works only for large text, and white text on the orange end of the gradient is unreadable, so button labels sit on the magenta side of the gradient.

All photos get one treatment: a warm color grade and a soft brand gradient, and in dark sections a magenta → orange duotone applied as a CSS filter. This makes inconsistent phone shots read as one series. AI (Figma Weave) only enhances the image (upscaling, light, color) and never alters people. We don't generate athletes.

Visual references: Ballerina (one warm color, whitespace, motion that feels like dance), Mougins en Danse (quiet premium feel, typography-led, plain JS), and Ballet Tech (a site grown out of its logo). For the header, KidStrong and Goldfish Swim School (one primary action), with The Little Gym as the anti-example with three competing buttons. For form behavior, the GOV.UK Design System: the error sits next to its field and tells the user how to fix it. From Fluid Functionalism we take the principles for button states and motion, and use its fluid hover highlight on desktop only.

## 5. Business Goals

The academy earns revenue from monthly tuition, and the site brings in new students. Money passes through the site at two points. The first is the $10 trial lesson (competitors charge $15–25), presented as a spot reservation. Per the SOW, the trial isn't paid online: the parent submits a request and the academy calls back. Online payment is our proposal, and the project lead decides on it `[confirm: D1]`. The second point is registration at `/register` after the trial, where the parent makes the first payment. The 2027 portal (`app.elyflame.com`) serves families who are already enrolled, for invoices and monthly payments; it isn't part of a new family's funnel. The site has no separate sales: adults interested in Stretching and families wanting private lessons also come in through the trial.

Prices follow the SOW: $10 for the trial and the price on the Stretching page. Children's program prices aren't published at launch. If the client allows it, we add a pricing block in phase 2 `[confirm: question 8]`. Whether the trial is paid at all (the `/free-trial` URL contradicts "Cost $10") and whether the $10 counts toward the first month is the client's call `[confirm: question 3]`.

We launch as an MVP on October 12, 2026. v1 includes five pages, the trial booking form with the class finder, registration, contacts, and legal pages. Not all client content may arrive by launch, so a block without content isn't shown on the site, and in the mockups each such block has two states: with content and hidden. We don't use "Coming soon" placeholders: parents read an empty spot as "this school isn't ready." We don't launch without a minimum: at least one coach with a photo and bio, the schedule, the address and phone number, and six or more real photos. Based on first-month results, we add a Russian page, Stretching landing pages, a live Instagram feed, and full online booking with payment.

The main metric, as in the SOW, is trial requests from the site per month. The target depends on open spots in classes `[confirm: D4]`. Alongside it, by November 12 we check three more numbers. The first is mobile visitor-to-request conversion; the first month sets the baseline. The second is the share of requests that come through the class finder, at least 50%, which validates the Champion's Path mechanic. The third is the share of trial families who complete registration `[confirm: current rate, D4]`. If registration is our own form, we count form submissions; if it's an external platform, the client provides the number. By January 12, 2027, we expect 30+ Google reviews, matching Rhythmix, the review leader in Buffalo Grove. The site affects reviews only through the link in the post-trial email. Without analytics, the first three metrics can't be measured `[confirm: D2]`. A mobile Lighthouse score of 90+ isn't a success metric; it's an SOW acceptance requirement (section 3).
